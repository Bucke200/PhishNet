— Why fiber

Fiber isn't faster cable. It's a different connection.

Three things change when the line to your house is fiber, and you'll notice all of them in the first week.

Speed

Headroom cable can't reach

Fiber runs well past what DSL or cable can deliver. That's the difference between one 4K stream working and four working while somebody's gaming and somebody else is on a call.

Symmetry

Uploads as fast as downloads

Almost every other connection type gives you a fast download and a crippled upload. Fiber gives you the same number both directions — which is what actually fixes choppy video calls and 40-minute file uploads.

Stability

Holds up at 8pm

Peak hours are when shared copper lines buckle. Fiber stays flat when everyone gets home, logs on, and hits play at the same time.

— Common questions

Everything you'd want to know first.

Fiber carries your data as pulses of light through hair-thin strands of glass, instead of the copper wiring cable and DSL run on. Light doesn't degrade over distance the way an electrical signal does — that's the entire reason fiber holds its speed where other connections fall off.
Three things change, and you feel all of them:
  • Speeds cable can't reach. Enough headroom for 4K on multiple screens, a full house of devices, and someone gaming at the same time.
  • Uploads that match downloads. Every other connection type gives you a fast download and a starved upload. Fiber gives you the same number both ways, which is what actually fixes video calls, big file transfers, and posting your own work online.
  • It doesn't sag at peak. When the whole street logs on at 8pm, fiber stays where it was at 2pm.
Fiber is the connection coming into the house. Wi-Fi is how that connection moves around inside it — wirelessly to your phone and laptop instead of through an ethernet cable. You need both, and the gateway that broadcasts your Wi-Fi comes with the plan.
Hyper-gig is the name for AT&T's fastest fiber tiers — the multi-gig plans, which also include AT&T All-Fi. Availability is limited to select areas, so the address check is what tells you whether hyper-gig is actually live on your street.
A technician usually needs to come out, even if you already have AT&T internet — fiber uses different cable and equipment than the copper running to most homes. Plan on roughly four to six hours. That window covers replacing the old copper run with fiber and getting your new gateway online. If you want extended coverage for a larger home, we can set that up at the same time.
